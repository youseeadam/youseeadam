## 🎬 My Journey: From the Stage to Datcenters and onward...

I've spent most of my career in IT and tech, but I took a pretty unconventional route to get here:

* 🎭 **The Early Days:** Having a BFA and MFA in Performing Arts Design, Technology, and Management, I worked on movies, TV shows, and live shows in theme parks. But honestly, live theater was always the most fun, and it is what I miss the most.
* 🌐 **Starting my own Company:** I co-founded a company doing commercials and websites. We were shooting local commercials and building websites (back when you had to know that a vertical line took longer to load than a horizontal line). My business partner wanted to lean into commercials, but I wanted to invest more in the internet. So, we decided to go our separate ways, and I moved back to my hometown in the Bay Area, California.
* 💾 **The IT Trenches:** I started in IT in the late '90s, crawling under desks, building PCs, and doing general service desk stuff. I was lucky to have a couple of mentors who showed me the ropes and gave me the opportunities I needed. I discovered that I had a knack for automation. I took processes that required hours of interactive, hands-on work and reduced them to minutes. This is when I discovered my passion for email systems, and I have survived every single Microsoft Exchange migration from 4.0 all the way through Exchange Online to M365. I spent a lot of my time working on Windows deployments, taking processes that would take hours of manual time down to minutes by leveraging automation and scripts. I spent time migrating from the NT4 domain days to LDAP and Active Directory infrastructures, and I worked heavily on integrating infrastructure during acquisitions. I really honed my ability to listen to user requirements in steering committees and translate them into a defined architecture for deployment.
* 🎤 **Finding My Voice:** At Aruba Networks, I led a massive "cut the cord" project, moving all desktop phones to IP/Softphones. Presenting this project to different groups sparked my absolute passion for Technical Marketing. When the project was done, sales teams at Aruba would bring me to conferences and shows to talk about it—both why we did it and the technical aspects. This is when I discovered my passion for Unified Communications and Solutions Engineering: helping others understand and find solutions to simplify collaboration. I spoke to large rooms of technical people and small meeting rooms of executives, all asking for my help. These conversations got me noticed in the Microsoft world and gave me the opportunity to write a couple of chapters for Skype for Business Unleashed. I started doing more presentations at events like CommsVNext, Ignite, and others. People come to listen to me, whether it is for my deep technical knowledge of UC, PowerShell, and email, or for my conceptual knowledge of meeting room design and data analytics.
* 📹 **The Logitech Years (Present):** When I had the opportunity to work for Logitech 7 years ago, I couldn't pass it up. I work in the Alliances group as the "Everything Else Guy." If we aren't sure how a partner fits into the Logitech Video Collaboration portfolio, I figure it out. I spend most of my time with API and Display Partners, and I'm proudly known as the "Windows PC Guy" (I build our Teams and Zoom Room images!).
